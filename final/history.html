The Story So Far

History of Space Exploration

Seven decades of launches, landings, and leaps of faith — the milestones that pushed humanity beyond its home planet.

Four Eras

The Ages of Spaceflight

The Space Race
1957 – 1969

A fierce Cold War competition between the USA and Soviet Union that produced the first satellites, the first human in space, and ultimately the Moon landings.

The Shuttle Era
1972 – 2011

The Space Shuttle made space feel routine — deploying telescopes, building the ISS, and flying 135 missions over 30 years.

The ISS Era
1998 – Present

International cooperation produced the largest structure ever assembled in space. Continuously inhabited for over two decades.

The Commercial Era
2010 – Present

Private companies like SpaceX disrupted the launch industry with reusable rockets, driving costs down and opening space to more missions.

Key Milestones

Mission Timeline

1957
Sputnik 1 — First Satellite

The Soviet Union launches the world's first artificial satellite on October 4th, inaugurating the Space Age.

1961
Vostok 1 — First Human in Space

Yuri Gagarin orbits Earth once in 108 minutes, becoming the first human to travel to space.

1969
Apollo 11 — First Moon Landing

Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin become the first humans to walk on the Moon.

1977
Voyager 1 and 2 — The Grand Tour

Twin probes launch to explore the outer solar system. Voyager 1 crosses into interstellar space in 2012.

1990
Hubble Space Telescope — A New View

Deployed by Space Shuttle Discovery, Hubble fundamentally changed our understanding of the universe.

2020
Crew Dragon — Commercial Crew Era

SpaceX carries NASA astronauts to the ISS from US soil for the first time since the Shuttle retired in 2011.

2021
James Webb Space Telescope — The Deepest View

JWST launches on Christmas Day and captures the deepest infrared images of the early universe ever taken.
